I have nearly 20 years of experience working with subsurface data in the oil and gas industry, across technical, software and product-facing roles. Why Microscope Lab

The petrographic microscope is where most geologists first meet rocks as minerals rather than lumps, and it is hard to get time on one. Microscope Lab puts a working one in the browser: rotate the stage, cross the polars, slide in the gypsum plate and watch grains go extinct. The interference colours are calculated from the physics, not painted on, so every grain sits where it should on the Michel-Lévy chart.

There are 21 thin sections: seven igneous, metamorphic, siliciclastic and evaporite rocks, and the 14 limestones and dolostones from Carbonate Lab.
