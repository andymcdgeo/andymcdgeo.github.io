Background

I have nearly 20 years of experience working with subsurface data in the oil and gas industry, across technical, software and product-facing roles. My work has included building industry software modules, developing applied machine learning workflows for geoscience problems, and creating technical training material for geoscientists.

I have delivered conference workshops, written long-form technical articles and published open notebooks on Python and subsurface data analysis. I also write independently through Subsurface Syntax, on practical ways of working with geological data.

Why Carbonate Lab

Carbonate rocks are usually taught from charts: Dunham's columns, Folk's grid of prefixes and suffixes. The charts hide the point, which is that every limestone is a record of the place it formed. Carbonate Lab puts the places back: a reef, a lagoon, an ooid shoal, a tidal flat and a salt flat, each with the rock forming there and its thin section. Every name in the lab is worked out by the same classification rules from what the generated thin section contains.
